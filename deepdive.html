Key Statistical Takeaways
1. Immigration & Diversity ($r = -0.75$): High immigrant and visible minority ridings vote overwhelmingly against separation.
2. Higher Education ($r = -0.66$): Ridings with high university degree attainment heavily favor remaining in Canada.

5. Three-Tier "Ticket-Splitting" & Coalition Drop-Off

Ballot Splitting

Albertans divided the 10 questions into three distinct ideological tiers: passing Immigration & Voter ID (Q1–Q5), rejecting Constitutional Overhaul (Q6–Q9), and overwhelmingly rejecting Separation (Q10 Option 2). Over half of Q1–Q5 "Yes" voters defected against Separation.

Tier 1: Immigration & Voter ID (Q1–Q5) ALL 5 PASSED
54.1% Won 42–62 of 87 ridings

Broad coalition: sweeps rural Alberta and wins up to 17/26 Calgary & 6/20 Edmonton ridings (on Q5 Voter ID).

Tier 2: Constitutional Overhaul (Q6–Q9) ALL 4 FAILED
44.9% Won 27–36 of 87 ridings

Suburban defection: loses ~9.2 pts province-wide vs. Tier 1 and gets shut out in Calgary (0/26 on Q6, Q8, Q9) and Edmonton (0/20).

Tier 3: Separation Referendum (Q10 Opt 2) REJECTED 3-TO-1
25.1% Won only 2 of 87 ridings

Massive 29-point drop-off from Tier 1: more than half of voters supporting Q1–Q5 voted to Remain in Canada.

6. Multivariate Demographic Model & Outlier Ridings

Model R² = 0.834

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) multiple regression combining 5 Census variables (Immigrant %, Bachelor's Degree %, Oil & Gas %, Homeownership %, and Average Age) explains 83.4% of the variance in Separation voting. Analyzing the residuals (Actual − Predicted) reveals which ridings defied their census demographics.

Top Overperformers (+Residual)

Southern rural ridings like 85 - Taber-Warner (+12.6 pts) and 54 - Cardston-Siksika (+10.6 pts) voted significantly higher for Separation than their age, education, and oil/gas share predict.

Top Underperformers (−Residual)

61 - Fort McMurray-Wood Buffalo (−11.9 pts) (40.6% actual vs. 52.5% predicted by its massive resource sector) and Edmonton suburbs St. Albert (−10.8 pts) and Sherwood Park (−9.8 pts) strongly favored remaining in Canada relative to their demographics.

7. Ballot Roll-Off & Selective Abstention

Up to 56,600 Skipped Ballots

Not every voter answered all 10 questions. Q10 (Separation) drew the highest participation on the ballot (2.069M valid votes), while 56,622 voters who cast a ballot on Q10 skipped Q7 (Abolish the Senate) and 41,049 skipped Q2.

8. The "Minority of Ridings, Majority of Votes" Paradox & Regional Net Margins

Electoral Geography

On Q2, Q3, and Q4, the "Yes" side won only 42 or 43 out of 87 ridings (a minority of electoral divisions!), yet passed province-wide (52.0%–53.1%) because huge lopsided surpluses in rural ridings outweighed narrower "No" majorities in Calgary and Edmonton.
